# R-32

R-32 (difluoromethane) is a single-component, low-GWP refrigerant emerging as one of two primary replacements for [[refrigerants/r-410a]] in U.S. residential AC and heat-pump equipment. The other leading replacement is [[refrigerants/r-454b]]. GWP is 675 (AR4), versus 2088 for R-410A [chunk_id: bulletin-r-32-transition-overview:0000:29e90451bd].

| Property | R-32 | R-410A |
|---|---|---|
| ASHRAE classification | A2L (mildly flammable) | A1 (non-flammable) |
| GWP (AR4) | 675 | 2088 |
| Single-component | Yes | No (blend) |
| Glide | None | Low |
| Approx. capacity vs R-410A | +5–10% | baseline |
| Saturation pressure | ~5–10% higher | baseline |

## regulatory_status

R-32 qualifies under the [[regulations/aim-act]] Technology Transitions Rule GWP < 700 cutoff. As of January 1, 2025, new manufacture and import of residential and light-commercial AC/HP equipment is restricted to refrigerants with GWP below 700; R-410A (GWP ~2088) does not qualify, while R-32 (GWP ~675) does [bulletin-epa-aim-act-summary:0000:2219085edc]. VRF equipment follows a slightly later cutoff phasing in by January 1, 2026 [bulletin-epa-aim-act-summary:0000:2219085edc].

R-32 is not itself subject to the bulk HFC phase-down allowance pressure in the same way R-410A is — it qualifies as a compliant refrigerant under the new equipment rules. However, the broader [[regulations/aim-act]] allowance schedule compresses overall HFC supply through 2028 and beyond, which continues to reshape the market context R-32 operates in [bulletin-epa-aim-act-summary:0000:2219085edc].

Service refills of existing R-410A systems remain legal after the 2025 manufacture cutoff; R-32 systems installed from 2025 onward will face their own service-refrigerant market over time [bulletin-epa-aim-act-summary:0000:2219085edc][bulletin-r-410a-phase-out-timeline:0000:ef1c9a2973].

## retrofit_options

R-32 is **not** a drop-in retrofit for existing [[refrigerants/r-410a]] systems. Mixing R-32 and R-410A in the same system destroys the system — strict separation of bulk inventory is required at the counter [bulletin-r-32-transition-overview:0000:29e90451bd].

R-32 is the replacement refrigerant of choice for new equipment from [[manufacturers/daikin]] (long-standing adoption), [[manufacturers/mitsubishi]], and parts of the [[manufacturers/goodman]] / [[manufacturers/amana]] lineup. [[manufacturers/carrier]], [[manufacturers/trane]], and [[manufacturers/lennox]] have primarily adopted [[refrigerants/r-454b]] for their residential lines, though Carrier's 2025 residential condenser catalog includes R-32 SKUs alongside R-454B [bulletin-r-32-transition-overview:0000:29e90451bd][spec-carrier-2025-residential-condensers:0000:730402755b]. [[manufacturers/rheem]] and [[manufacturers/york]] show partial adoption of R-32 across their lines [bulletin-r-454b-overview:0000:5abf4e6fa7][spec-rheem-2025-r-454b-condensers:0000:bb87b1f4eb]. [[manufacturers/trane]]'s 2024 commercial catalog also includes R-32 condenser SKUs [spec-trane-2024-commercial-r-32:0000:531e8bb392].

For contractors transitioning off R-410A equipment, the practical retrofit path is replacement with a new R-32 or R-454B unit rather than any refrigerant conversion of the existing system. See [[procedures/r-410a-retrofit-workflow]] for the full workflow. Distributor stocking of both R-32 and R-454B in parallel is required through the transition period, with OEM choice verified on every supplier order [bulletin-r-32-transition-overview:0000:29e90451bd].

Line set reuse from R-410A systems is permitted for R-32 if lengths are within OEM spec, the line set is free of moisture and acid, and the line set is triple-flushed per OEM procedure [manual-synth-install-residential-condenser:0000:dc2822e656].

Forward pre-buys on R-32 are not currently offered by this distributor, as supply is not constrained. Revisit if supplier allocation notices appear [sop-refrigerant-pre-buy-guidance:0000:75588b7bfe].

## exposure_summary

R-32's A2L (mildly flammable) classification is the primary operational exposure. It is not dangerous in normal residential charges, but it does drive specific requirements [bulletin-r-32-transition-overview:0000:29e90451bd]:

- **Leak detection:** A2L-rated leak detectors required in confined spaces.
- **Recovery and gauging:** recovery machines and manifold gauges must be A2L-rated.
- **Brazing:** torches are still permitted but must follow manufacturer-specified procedures.
- **Charge limits:** UL 60335-2-40 charge limits apply; minimum room volume for the evaporator location must be confirmed at site survey [manual-synth-install-residential-condenser:0000:dc2822e656].
- **Installer training:** A2L classification drives additional training requirements beyond what was needed for R-410A [bulletin-r-32-transition-overview:0000:29e90451bd].

Inventory separation is a hard operational requirement — R-32 and R-410A cylinders must never be co-mingled, and cross-contamination of a system is catastrophic [bulletin-r-32-transition-overview:0000:29e90451bd]. Counter staff and warehouse personnel need clear labeling and bin separation protocols in place before R-32 volume picks up.

From a distributor SKU-exposure standpoint, stocking two replacement refrigerants (R-32 and R-454B) in parallel through the transition increases complexity. R-410A service-refill pricing is projected to climb 20–60% through the allowance-compression period, making proactive contractor outreach on replacement equipment a priority [bulletin-r-410a-phase-out-timeline:0000:ef1c9a2973][sop-r-410a-retrofit-customer-outreach:0000:2349bfb54c].
