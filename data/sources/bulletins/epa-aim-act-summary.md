# AIM Act HFC phase-down — operational summary

This is a paraphrased operational summary of the EPA American Innovation
and Manufacturing Act (AIM Act). It is not the original regulatory text.
For the authoritative version see the EPA Final Rule (40 CFR Part 84).

## What the AIM Act does

The AIM Act phases down the U.S. production and import of
hydrofluorocarbon (HFC) refrigerants on a fixed allowance schedule. The
phase-down is set against a baseline of average HFC consumption from
2011 to 2013.

| Period      | Allowance vs baseline |
| ----------- | --------------------- |
| 2022–2023   | 90%                   |
| 2024–2028   | 60%                   |
| 2029–2033   | 30%                   |
| 2034–2035   | 20%                   |
| 2036+       | 15%                   |

Allowances are auctioned and traded annually. Manufacturers must hold
allowances to produce or import bulk HFCs.

## Equipment-specific cutoffs (Technology Transitions Rule)

Separate from the bulk-refrigerant phase-down, the EPA Technology
Transitions Rule restricts the GWP of refrigerants used in **new**
equipment, by equipment category and date:

- **Residential and light commercial AC and heat pumps:** new
  manufacture restricted to refrigerants with GWP < 700 starting
  **January 1, 2025**. R-410A (GWP ~2088) does not qualify; common
  replacements are R-32 (GWP ~675) and R-454B (GWP ~466).
- **Variable refrigerant flow (VRF):** GWP < 700 cutoff phases in by
  **January 1, 2026**.
- **Chillers, industrial process refrigeration:** category-specific
  dates between 2025 and 2027.

Service and existing-equipment refills of R-410A remain legal after the
2025 manufacture cutoff. Allowance pressure tightens supply each year.

## Operational implications for distributors

- R-410A equipment **already in inventory** on 2024-12-31 may be sold
  and installed in the U.S. after the cutoff — the rule restricts
  manufacture and import, not the sell-through of existing stock.
- R-410A bulk refrigerant for service is expected to tighten in price
  through 2026 as allowances compress.
- New residential and light-commercial product lines from major OEMs
  (Carrier, Trane, Lennox, Goodman) are shifting to R-32 or R-454B for
  the 2025 model year.
